Nothering is the iPhone half of a two-part app. The iPhone app runs a SOCKS5 proxy on port 11080, and the companion Mac app (free, open source, distributed outside the Mac App Store at https://github.com/devxoul/nothering/releases) sends the Mac's traffic to it over USB or Personal Hotspot. The iPhone opens each connection over cellular.

How to test:
1. On the iPhone, open Nothering and tap Start Proxy. The status changes to Running.
2. Install the Mac app from the link above, connect the Mac to the iPhone with a USB cable (or join the iPhone's Personal Hotspot), and turn on Nothering from the menu bar icon.
3. Load a website on the Mac. The iPhone app shows "Mac connected via USB" and the throughput chart moves.

Without a Mac, you can check the proxy from the iPhone side only: after tapping Start Proxy the app shows Running and "Waiting for Mac...".

Background audio: the proxy has to keep accepting connections from the Mac while the iPhone is locked, which is the app's core feature. iOS gives a network proxy no other way to stay running, so the app plays silent audio only while the proxy is on. A Live Activity makes it clear to the user that the proxy is running, and tapping Stop Proxy ends the audio session immediately.

The proxy only accepts connections from the USB link (loopback) and the Personal Hotspot interface, never from Wi-Fi or cellular peers. The app has no accounts and collects no data.
